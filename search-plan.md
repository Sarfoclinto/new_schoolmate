In Ghana, most "POS systems" businesses use are either imported (QuickBooks, Odoo, SAP) which cost thousands of dollars per year, or very basic local apps with limited features. You are sitting between those two — a custom-built, locally-hosted, Ghanaian-context system. That is your competitive advantage.

Pricing Models — Know These Terms
There are three ways to charge for software:

1. One-Time License Fee (Build + Deploy)
Client pays once. You build, deploy, hand over. They own it. You have no ongoing obligation unless they pay for support.

Pros: Money upfront, clean transaction Cons: No recurring income, clients call you for every bug forever if you don't set boundaries

2. SaaS / Subscription (Monthly/Annual)
Client pays monthly or yearly to use the system. You host it, maintain it, update it.

Pros: Predictable recurring income, scales with clients Cons: You carry hosting costs, support burden

3. Hybrid (Setup Fee + Monthly Retainer)
Client pays a setup/deployment fee once, then a smaller monthly fee for hosting, updates, and support.

This is what I recommend for you. It's the most common model for local software vendors in Ghana.

What to Charge — Ghana Market Rates
Reference points in the Ghanaian market:
A basic POS with just sales recording: GHS 3,000 – 8,000 one-time
Mid-range inventory + sales system: GHS 12,000 – 30,000 one-time
Enterprise multi-store system (Odoo localisation etc.): GHS 40,000 – 150,000+
Monthly SaaS (local competitors like mPOS Ghana, etc.): GHS 300 – 800/month
Monthly SaaS (international, SME tier): $30 – $150/month (~GHS 450 – 2,200)
Where your system sits:
You are squarely in the mid-to-upper-mid range. The multi-store, multi-role, real-time, report-heavy nature of what you built pushes it there. But since you are unknown and this is your first commercial deployment, you should price slightly below your actual value to win your first 2–3 clients, then raise rates.

What "Monthly Support" Actually Covers
Be explicit with every client. Write this down and make them sign it. Monthly support should cover:

✅ Server/hosting uptime (you pay the VPS/cloud bill from this)
✅ Bug fixes for existing features
✅ Minor UI tweaks (not new features)
✅ Database backups
✅ Security patches
✅ WhatsApp/phone support (define hours — e.g. Mon–Fri 8am–6pm)

❌ New features (charged separately)
❌ Hardware problems (printers, receipt paper, their internet)
❌ Data they deleted themselves
❌ Training new staff (charged separately)

Contract Terms — Do This Every Time
Never work without a written agreement. It does not need to be from a lawyer to be effective. It should cover:

Scope — exactly what is included. List every module by name.
Payment terms — 50% upfront, 50% on delivery. Never start work with zero payment.
Monthly support terms — what is and is not included (as above)
Data ownership — they own their data, you own the code
Termination — if they stop paying monthly, hosting goes down after 30 days notice
Confidentiality — you won't share their business data
Updates — major version updates are included in monthly; platform rewrites are not
Practical Advice for Your First Client
Since you have no portfolio, your first client is about proof, not profit. Consider:

Offer a 3-month free trial in exchange for a testimonial and referral
Or offer 50% off the setup fee for the first client, full monthly rate after month 1
Deploy it at a friend's or family member's shop first so you can show a live system to prospects
Take photos/video of it running in a real store — that is your pitch deck
